Title: Angle Between Hands of a Clock
URL: https://leetcode.com/problems/angle-between-hands-of-a-clock/

LLM Explanation:
The problem asks for the smaller angle between the hour and minute hands of a clock. The solution involves calculating the angular position of each hand relative to a common reference point (the 12 o'clock mark) and then finding the smaller of the two angles between them.

### Step-by-Step Logic:

1.  **Angle of the Minute Hand:**
    *   A clock face is a 360° circle.
    *   The minute hand completes a full 360° rotation in 60 minutes.
    *   Therefore, the angle it moves per minute is `360° / 60 = 6°`.
    *   The position of the minute hand, measured clockwise from 12, is calculated as:
        `minute_angle = minutes * 6.0`

2.  **Angle of the Hour Hand:**
    *   The hour hand completes a full 360° rotation in 12 hours.
    *   The angle between two adjacent hour marks is `360° / 12 = 30°`.
    *   The hour hand's position is not static; it moves continuously as the minutes pass. In 60 minutes, it moves from one hour mark to the next (a 30° change).
    *   Therefore, the additional angle it moves per minute is `30° / 60 = 0.5°`.
    *   To simplify calculations, we can treat 12 o'clock as 0. The expression `hour % 12` achieves this (e.g., `12 % 12 = 0`, while `3 % 12 = 3`).
    *   The total position of the hour hand, measured clockwise from 12, is the sum of the angle from the hour value and the angle from the minutes value:
        `hour_angle = (hour % 12) * 30.0 + minutes * 0.5`

3.  **Finding the Smaller Angle:**
    *   The absolute difference between the two angles gives one of the angles formed by the hands:
        `angle_diff = abs(hour_angle - minute_angle)`
    *   The other angle is the reflex angle, which is `360 - angle_diff`.
    *   The problem requires the smaller of these two angles.
    *   The final result is:
        `min(angle_diff, 360 - angle_diff)`

### Complexity Analysis:

*   **Time Complexity:** O(1), as the solution involves a constant number of arithmetic operations.
*   **Space Complexity:** O(1), as it uses a fixed amount of memory regardless of the input.
