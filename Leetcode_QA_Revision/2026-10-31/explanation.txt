Title: Twisted Mirror Path Count
URL: https://leetcode.com/problems/twisted-mirror-path-count/

LLM Explanation:
The earlier version was wrong because it put the landing cell of a reflection in the wrong place and mishandled the chaining of mirrors. A mirror hit while moving right sends the robot to the cell directly below that mirror, not to a diagonal cell.

The fix is to keep two counts for every cell, including mirror cells: A[i][j] is the number of ways the robot enters (i,j) while moving right, and B[i][j] is the number of ways it enters while moving down.
- A non-mirror cell has T = A + B ways to be occupied. From it the robot can go right, which adds T to A[i][j+1], or down, which adds T to B[i+1][j].
- A mirror cell is never occupied. Ways that entered it moving right are turned down, adding A to B[i+1][j]. Ways that entered it moving down are turned right, adding B to A[i][j+1].
- Chains of mirrors need no special handling, because a reflected move enters the next cell with its new direction, and that cell applies the same rule. Each cell's A and B are final by the time the row-major scan reaches it.
- Reflections that leave the grid land in the padding row or column and are never read, so they are dropped.
- The answer is A + B at the bottom-right cell, taken modulo 10^9+7.

Time complexity: O(m*n). Space complexity: O(m*n).

Edge cases:
- Reflection out of bounds is not counted.
- Long diagonal chains of mirrors resolve cell by cell.
- Start and end cells are guaranteed to be 0.
- The start is seeded as A[0][0]=1 and acts as a free cell with T=1.
